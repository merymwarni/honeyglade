PART 01

What Is Descriptive Text?

Let's understand the basic concept!

📚

Get to Know Descriptive Text

Descriptive text is a type of text that describes a particular person, animal, place, or thing in detail. It helps readers imagine what something or someone looks like.

In this lesson, you will learn how to describe characters, especially Winnie the Pooh and his friends.

💡

Remember! Descriptive text helps readers imagine a subject through its appearance, characteristics, and personality.

PART 02

What Will You Learn?

These are your learning goals for this lesson.

🔍

Identify

Identify the generic structure of descriptive text.

📝

Recognize

Recognize the language features of descriptive text.

✏️

Create

Write a simple descriptive text about a character.

PART 03

Social Function

Why do we use descriptive text?

🌳

The Purpose of Descriptive Text

The social function of descriptive text is to describe a particular person, animal, place, or thing clearly and specifically so that readers can understand its characteristics.

PART 04

Generic Structure

A descriptive text has two main parts.

👋 PART ONE

1. Identification

Introduces the person, animal, place, or thing that will be described.

Example:

Winnie the Pooh is a famous teddy bear who lives in the Hundred Acre Wood.

🔎 PART TWO

2. Description

Describes the characteristics, appearance, personality, or other details of the subject.

Example:

He has yellow fur, wears a red shirt, and loves eating honey. He is friendly, kind, and cheerful.

PART 05

Language Features

Let's discover the language used in descriptive text!

⏰

1. Simple Present Tense

Used to describe general facts and characteristics.

Example:

Pooh loves honey.

🎨

2. Adjectives

Words used to describe the characteristics of a subject.

Examples:

Cute, friendly, kind, yellow, and cheerful.

🐻

3. Specific Participants

Focuses on a particular person, animal, place, or thing.

Examples:

Winnie the Pooh, Piglet, and Rabbit.

🔗

4. Linking Verbs

Words such as is, am, and are connect the subject with its description.

Example:

Pooh is a friendly bear.

PART 01

What Is Narrative Text?

Let's discover the world of storytelling!

📚

Get to Know Narrative Text

Narrative text is a type of text that tells a story or a series of events. It can be a fictional or imaginative story designed to entertain readers and sometimes teach a moral lesson.

Stories about Winnie the Pooh and his friends can help us understand how a narrative text is organized.

PART 02

What Will You Learn?

Here are your learning goals for this lesson.

🎯

Understand

Understand the definition and social function of narrative text.

🔍

Identify

Identify the generic structure of narrative text.

📝

Recognize

Recognize common language features in narrative text.

💡

Understand the Message

Understand the message or moral value of a story.

PART 03

Social Function

Why do we read narrative texts?

🌳

The Purpose of Narrative Text

The social function of narrative text is to entertain readers through a story and provide a message or moral lesson.

PART 04

Generic Structure

Let's understand the four parts of a narrative text!

🌞 PART ONE

1. Orientation

Introduces the characters, setting, and situation of the story.

Example:

One sunny morning, Pooh and Piglet walked through the forest.

🌧️ PART TWO

2. Complication

Introduces a problem or conflict faced by the characters.

Example:

Suddenly, they could not find the way back home.

🤝 PART THREE

3. Resolution

Explains how the characters solve the problem.

Example:

Rabbit helped them find the correct path.

🌼 PART FOUR

4. Re-orientation

OPTIONAL

Provides a closing statement or lesson from the story.

Example:

Pooh learned that helping one another makes friendship stronger.

💡

Remember! Re-orientation is optional. Not every narrative text includes this final part.

PART 05

Language Features

Discover the language commonly used in narrative text!

⏰

1. Simple Past Tense

Used to tell events that happened in the past.

Example:

Pooh walked through the forest.

🏃

2. Action Verbs

Words that show actions performed by the characters.

Examples:

Walked, ran, found, helped, and played.

🔗

3. Time Connectives

Words used to show the order of events.

Examples:

Once upon a time, one day, then, and finally.

🐻

4. Specific Participants

Uses particular characters in the story.

Examples:

Winnie the Pooh, Piglet, and Tigger.

GRAMMAR • TENSES

Simple Present Tense

Learn to express habits, routines, and general facts.

01

What Is Simple Present Tense?

📖

Simple Present Tense is used to talk about habits, daily routines, general facts, and things that are usually true.

We can use this tense to describe the daily activities and characteristics of Winnie the Pooh and his friends.

02

What Will You Learn?

🎯

Understand the use of Simple Present Tense.

✏️

Recognize affirmative, negative, and interrogative sentences.

📚

Use the correct verb forms in sentences.

💡

Create simple sentences about daily activities.

03

Functions of Simple Present Tense

🌞

1. Daily Routines

Used to describe activities that happen regularly.

Example:

Pooh eats honey every morning.

🔁

2. Habits

Used to describe actions that someone does repeatedly.

Example:

Piglet visits Pooh every day.

🌿

3. General Facts

Used to describe facts that are generally true.

Example:

Bees make honey.

04

Sentence Patterns

+

1. Affirmative (Positive)

Subject + Verb 1 (s/es) + Object/Complement
Examples:

Pooh loves honey.

Piglet walks in the forest.

−

2. Negative

Subject + do/does + not + Verb 1 + Object/Complement
Examples:

Pooh does not like vegetables.

Piglet does not eat honey every day.

?

3. Interrogative

Do/Does + Subject + Verb 1 + Object/Complement?
Examples:

Does Pooh love honey?

Do the friends play together?

05

Important Grammar Notes

🔤

Use of -s and -es

For he, she, and it, we usually add -s or -es to the main verb in affirmative sentences.

Examples:

He eats honey.

She watches television.

📝

Do and Does

Use do with I, you, we, and they. Use does with he, she, and it.

Examples:

They do not run.

He does not run.

06

Example Sentences

🍯 A LITTLE STORY

A Day with Pooh

🌞 Pooh wakes up early every morning.

🍯 He looks for honey in the forest.

🐷 Piglet visits Pooh in the afternoon.

🌳 They play together and enjoy their day.

💡 Notice the verbs! They describe habits and daily routines.

GRAMMAR • TENSES

Present Continuous Tense

Learn how to describe actions happening right now.

01

What Is Present Continuous Tense?

📖

Present Continuous Tense is used to describe actions that are happening at the moment of speaking or around the present time.

It helps us explain what Winnie the Pooh and his friends are doing right now.

02

What Will You Learn?

🎯

Understand the use of Present Continuous Tense.

✏️

Recognize the correct sentence pattern.

📚

Use am, is, and are with verb-ing.

💡

Describe actions happening now.

03

Functions of Present Continuous Tense

⏰

1. Actions Happening Now

Used to describe something happening at the moment.

Example:

Pooh is eating honey now.

📅

2. Temporary Activities

Used to describe activities happening around the present time.

Example:

Piglet is staying with Pooh this week.

04

Sentence Patterns

+

1. Affirmative (Positive)

Subject + am/is/are + Verb-ing + Object/Complement
Examples:

Pooh is eating honey.

They are playing in the forest.

−

2. Negative

Subject + am/is/are + not + Verb-ing + Object/Complement
Examples:

Pooh is not sleeping.

They are not running.

?

3. Interrogative

Am/Is/Are + Subject + Verb-ing + Object/Complement?
Examples:

Is Pooh eating honey?

Are the friends playing?

05

Use of Am, Is, and Are

🙋

AM

I

Used with the subject I.

Example:

I am reading a story.

🐻

IS

He • She • It

Used with he, she, and it.

Example:

Pooh is eating honey.

🐷

ARE

You • We • They

Used with you, we, and they.

Example:

They are playing together.

06

Example Sentences

🍯 A LITTLE STORY

What Are Pooh and His Friends Doing?

🍯 Pooh is eating honey.

🐷 Piglet is walking in the forest.

🐯 Tigger is jumping happily.

🥕 Rabbit is planting vegetables.

💡 Notice the am, is, are + verb-ing forms! They describe actions happening now.

Simple Past Tense

Learn how to talk about completed actions in the past.

01

What Is Simple Past Tense?

📖

Simple Past Tense is used to talk about actions or events that happened and finished in the past.

We can use this tense to tell stories about the adventures of Winnie the Pooh and his friends.

02

What Will You Learn?

🎯

Understand the use of Simple Past Tense.

📚

Recognize regular and irregular verbs.

✏️

Create affirmative, negative, and interrogative sentences.

💡

Talk about past activities and experiences.

03

Functions of Simple Past Tense

✅

1. Completed Actions

Used to describe actions that finished in the past.

Example:

Pooh ate honey yesterday.

🕰️

2. Past Events

Used to tell events that happened at a specific time in the past.

Example:

Piglet visited Pooh last Sunday.

04

Sentence Patterns

+

1. Affirmative (Positive)

Subject + Verb 2 + Object/Complement
Examples:

Pooh ate honey yesterday.

Piglet visited his friend last week.

−

2. Negative

Subject + did not + Verb 1 + Object/Complement
Examples:

Pooh did not eat honey yesterday.

Piglet did not visit Rabbit.

?

3. Interrogative

Did + Subject + Verb 1 + Object/Complement?
Examples:

Did Pooh eat honey yesterday?

Did Piglet visit Rabbit?

05

Regular and Irregular Verbs

🔤

Regular Verbs

Regular verbs usually add -ed or -d to form the past tense.

Walk Walked
Play Played
Visit Visited
✨

Irregular Verbs

Irregular verbs change their forms in the past tense.

Eat Ate
Go Went
See Saw
💡 Remember: Use Verb 2 in affirmative sentences, but use Verb 1 after did or did not.
06

🌳 They walked through the forest.

🏡 Finally, they went home happily.

💡 Notice the past verbs! They tell us about activities that happened and finished yesterday.

GRAMMAR • FUTURE TENSE

Be Going To

Learn how to express future plans and intentions through the exciting adventures of Winnie the Pooh and his friends!

Let's Plan an Adventure!
01

What Is Be Going To?

Get to know the basic concept

💡

Let's Understand!

Be going to is used to talk about future plans, intentions, and actions that someone has already decided to do.

We can use it to talk about the future plans of Winnie the Pooh and his friends!

02

What Will You Learn?

Your learning goals

📖

Understand the use of be going to.

📝

Recognize the correct sentence pattern.

🔤

Use am, is, and are correctly.

🌟

Express future plans and intentions.

03

Function of Be Going To

When do we use it?

🗓️

1. Future Plans

Used to describe plans that someone has already decided to do.

Example

Pooh is going to visit Piglet tomorrow.

🎯

2. Future Intentions

Used to express an intention to do something in the future.

Example

Tigger is going to help his friends.

04

Sentence Patterns

Learn how to make different sentences

+

Affirmative (Positive)

Subject + am/is/are + going to + Verb 1 + Object

🐻 Pooh is going to eat honey.

🐷 They are going to play together.

−

Negative

Subject + am/is/are + not + going to + Verb 1 + Object

🐻 Pooh is not going to sleep.

🐷 They are not going to stay at home.

?

Interrogative (Question)

Am/Is/Are + Subject + going to + Verb 1 + Object?

🐻 Is Pooh going to eat honey?

🐷 Are they going to play together?

05

Use of Am, Is, and Are

Choose the correct helping verb

🙋

AM

I

I am going to read a story.

🐻

IS

He • She • It

Pooh is going to visit Piglet.

🐯

ARE

You • We • They

They are going to explore the forest.

⭐ Remember!

Always use am, is, or are before going to. After going to, use the base form of the verb (Verb 1).

06
